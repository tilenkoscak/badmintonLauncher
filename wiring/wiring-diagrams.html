Five rules that apply to every drawing.
  1. The ESP32 is 3.3 V logic and not 5 V tolerant. Nothing above 3.3 V may reach a GPIO.
  2. One common ground: every GND meets at the star point next to the PSU. Without it STEP, DIR and PWM have no reference.
  3. Never plug or unplug a stepper while its driver has 24 V. It kills the DRV8825 at once.
  4. The wheel motors are 12 V. Until the 12 V buck is fitted the wheel rail is 24 V and every wheel test stays at or below 50 % duty.
  5. Motor current never flows through the ESP32 board or a breadboard. Wire one subsystem at a time and run its smoke test first.

1System overview

The phone is only a remote. The ESP32 runs the Wi-Fi access point, serves the web app and owns every motor and every safety rule.

2The ESP32-S3 board and what goes on each pin

Tilen's board, held with the components facing you, the antenna up and the USB-C ports down. Labels inside the board are the silkscreen; labels outside are what the pin is used for. Hover a pin for details.

From 01-esp32-s3-basics.md and pin-map.md. Header matches the Espressif DevKitC-1 v1.1 label for label; pin 1 of each row is at the antenna end.

Which USB-C port is which? They are not labelled on the top side. The USB-serial chip sits next to the port on the J3 side, so that one is most likely COM (uploads and serial monitor) and the other the native USB port. To be sure, plug in one port at a time: the native port adds a USB JTAG/serial debug unit in Windows Device Manager next to its COM port; the COM port does not. Smoke test 1 prints only on the COM port.
Never connect anything to: GPIO 35, 36, 37 (octal PSRAM of the N16R8, even though they are on the header), 19, 20 (USB), TX/RX = 43, 44 (serial). Keep the strapping pins 0, 3, 45, 46 free: a driver pulling one of them at reset stops the board from booting.

4Steppers: pan, tilt and feeder carriage through DRV8825

Three identical driver circuits on the 24 V rail. Only STEP and DIR differ between them. The drawing shows the pan driver.

From 02-steppers-drv8825.md.

How the three drivers share the ESP32 pins

Microstepping and gearing

AxisSTEP / DIRGearingMicrosteps per degree
Pan4 / 5≈ 1:36.57 planetary≈ 162.5
Tilt6 / 71:72320
Feeder15 / 16taught in steps1600 per motor rev

All three drivers at 1/8: M0 and M1 to 3V3, M2 open. Positions are stored in microsteps, so the jumpers must not change after teaching.

Current limit

Vref 0.6 V on all three drivers, measured on the prototype. With the usual R100 sense resistors that is 1.2 A per phase. The motor's rated current is not published, so watch the temperature after the first long session: too hot to hold means turn Vref down by 0.05 V.
Before wiring: RESET and SLEEP were probably on the Uno's 5 V. On the ESP32 they go to 3V3, otherwise FAULT idles at 5 V.

5Feeder servos: arms and lift

Two Miuzei 15 kg servos on their own 5-6 V rail, never on the ESP32's regulator. Only the signal wires and one ground go to the ESP32.

From 03-servos.md.

ServoGPIOPositionsSettle (default)
Arms10OPEN 145°, CLOSED 154°300 ms
Lift11BOTTOM 160°, TOP 0°800 ms
Angles and pulse range travel together. These angles use the Uno Servo scale, 0-180° = 544-2400 µs, and the firmware uses the same range. Arms OPEN and CLOSED are only 9° apart, so a different range would press the arms into the cork. Commands are clamped to 0-160°.

6Launch wheels: buck, MOSFET module, 775 motor

One branch per wheel; wheel L shown. Wheel R is identical with GPIO 13 and its own fuse, module, capacitor, TVS and diode. The buck and the E-STOP are shared.

From 04-launch-wheels-dc-motors.md.

Safety, non-negotiable. No disks on the motors for the first power tests. The web app's E-STOP and the connection watchdog must work before the disks go back on. The physical E-STOP must be reachable without a hand near the wheels.
Rail voltage. Until the 12 V buck is fitted the rail is 24 V and the firmware caps the duty at 50 % (WHEEL_RAIL_V = 24). Set it to 12 only after the new buck is fitted and measured.

Wiring: 2.5 mm² twisted pair per motor, short, away from the ESP32 and its antenna. 1000 µF and TVS right at the module's screw terminals. Both modules on heatsinks; the E83-004 tab is the cathode, so use insulating washers on a shared heatsink.

7Endstop sensor: home of the feeder carriage

The sensor salvaged from the printer homes the feeder carriage. It is powered and drives its signal itself: LOW when triggered (Uno code). On the Uno it ran from 5 V, which must not reach an ESP32 pin.

From 06-optical-endstop.md.

Not a slotted optical endstop. The photo (2026-10-23) shows a small sensor body with a round sensing face, an LED and a three-wire cable: most likely the Sidewinder X3 Plus's inductive bed-levelling sensor. It reacts to steel a few millimetres in front of the round face. Cardboard, plastic, a finger or pressing on it do nothing, which is why the earlier tests never switched.

Bench test: Uno + multimeter, no ESP32

Steps
  1. Sensor unplugged from everything else; Uno on USB.
  2. Brown to 3.3V, blue to GND. The black wire touches nothing but the red probe.
  3. Meter on DC volts, 20 V; leads in COM and V. Black probe on the Uno's other GND, red probe on the black wire.
  4. Note the reading with nothing near the round face. Then hold steel flat against the face and note it again. Watch the LED.
  5. Repeat with brown on 5V instead of 3.3V.
Expected when it works: two clearly different, steady values, one near 0 V. Send all four numbers.
Common mistakes
  • Testing with cardboard, plastic or a finger: this sensor only sees metal.
  • Steel too far away: hold it flat, touching the round face or within 1-2 mm.
  • Meter on AC volts (V with a wavy line) instead of DC volts (V with a straight line).
  • Red lead in the 10A jack instead of the V jack.
  • Black probe on something other than the Uno's GND: the meter, the sensor and the Uno must share the same ground.

Connection to the ESP32 (after the bench test)

Position: at the front of the carriage travel, towards the wheels. It marks the furthest the feeder may go: the shuttle reaches the flywheels and the mechanism does not run into them. Homing therefore moves towards the wheels and only runs with the wheels off. WHEELS sits at the release point or just behind it.
Still open: the bench test with steel on 3.3 V and 5 V, the wire colours, and what on the prototype's carriage triggered it. If it is the inductive sensor, the new carriage needs a piece of steel that passes within about 1-2 mm of the round face at the home position.

8Power distribution

The Artillery Sidewinder X3 Plus PSU has two outputs: 24 V for aiming, feeding and logic, and 36 V that becomes the wheel rail.

From 05-power.md.

★ Star point next to the PSU. The two PSU outputs are isolated from each other (measured), so this is the only link between the 24 V side and the wheel side: without it the wheel PWM has no reference. One wire each from: PSU output 1 -, PSU output 2 -, wheel buck -, both MOSFET modules DC-, the three DRV8825 motor GNDs, N7805 GND, servo buck GND and one ESP32 GND pin.
Power-up order: ESP32 first (it is up as soon as the PSU is), then the wheel switch. Power-down in reverse. The ENABLE pull-up and the wheel PWM pull-downs keep everything off if the order is not respected.

9Build order

One subsystem at a time; each step ends with the smoke test in its .md file.

  1. ESP32 alone on USB: LED blink, serial, PSRAM check, Wi-Fi scan. Find out which port is COM and which GPIO the RGB LED is on. 01-esp32-s3-basics.md
  2. Endstop sensor: bench test with the Uno and a multimeter (section 7), then on the ESP32: confirm it reads LOW with steel in front of it. 06-optical-endstop.md
  3. One DRV8825 + pan stepper on the 24 V rail, then tilt, then feeder. 02-steppers-drv8825.md
  4. Both servos on their own 5-6 V rail; check the four angles. 03-servos.md
  5. Launch wheels with bare shafts, ideally after the 12 V buck is fitted and measured. 04-launch-wheels-dc-motors.md
  6. Final power distribution, fuses, E-STOP. 05-power.md
